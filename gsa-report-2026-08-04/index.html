Total ad spend for the 30 days was $146.31 — an average of $4.88 a day. For that, 68,355 people in an eight-mile radius saw Hoys Wok at the top of their search results, 1,525 of them clicked through to your ordering page, and 2,056 took a follow-up action like pulling up directions or opening the menu.

The single biggest change this period: a full account tune-up on July 24 cut wasted ad impressions by 42% while holding your click volume completely flat. You are now paying to appear in front of a much more relevant audience for the same money.

What's working

Three results worth calling out

Each of these compares the ten days after the July 24 tune-up with the ten days immediately before it, so the comparison is like-for-like.

+72%

More people click when they see you

Click-through rate went from 1.84% to 3.17%. Roughly one in every 32 people who see your ad now clicks it — well above the restaurant average.

−42%

Far less wasted exposure

Daily impressions dropped from 2,796 to 1,627 — on purpose. We removed searches that were never going to order from you, like competitor names and dishes you don't serve.

$0.096

Clicks stayed remarkably cheap

Under ten cents a visit, with click volume unchanged at about 52 a day. The tighter targeting didn't cost you traffic — it just cost you less irrelevant traffic.

Ad performance

The new ad is beating the old one by a wide margin

We replaced the campaign's search ad on July 24 with fresh headlines and descriptions built around online ordering, lunch specials and family meals. Both versions ran within the reporting period, so we can compare them directly.

Demand pattern

When your customers are searching

Search demand tracks your service day almost exactly: a lunch bump around noon and a strong dinner peak from 4pm to 6pm. Ads currently run 8am to 11pm, seven days a week.

Nearly nine in ten of your clicks come from a phone — 1,359 of 1,525. Mobile searchers click your ad at 2.49%, more than double the 1.14% rate on desktop. That is exactly the behaviour you want to see for a neighbourhood takeout restaurant: people are searching while they're out and hungry.

Work completed

What we changed on July 24

A single tune-up session, all of it aimed at spending your budget on people who can actually become customers.

24 search terms blocked
Competitor names (Panda Express, Din Tai Fung, Dan Modern), dishes not on your menu (hot pot, dim sum, pho, buffet), out-of-area towns (Downey, Paramount, El Monte, Monterey Park) and non-customer searches (recipes, jobs, hiring).
4 broad keywords paused
Including "restaurants los angeles" and "chinese food places" — high volume, but too generic to bring in orders. Between them they had absorbed about $87 of spend before being paused, and have cost nothing since.
New search ad written and launched
Rebuilt around online ordering, lunch specials, family meals and your 1985 heritage. Now running at a 3.11% click-through rate.
10 ad extensions added
Four quick links (Order Online, Family Meals, Lunch Specials, Delivery), four callouts (Serving LA Since 1985, Pickup and Delivery, Fresh Made To Order, Order Online) and two dish/amenity lists. These make your ad physically larger on the results page.
Ad hours set to 8am–11pm
Stops the campaign paying for overnight searches when you're closed. Overnight spend has been zero since the schedule went in; before it, roughly $10 a month was going to hours between midnight and 8am.
Targeting held to an 8-mile radius
Keeps spend on searchers close enough to actually pick up or receive delivery.
